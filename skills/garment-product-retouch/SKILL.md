# Garment Product Retouch

## Priority

Product accuracy is more important than visual perfection. Do not make a real garment look like a different product.

## Protected attributes

Unless explicitly requested, preserve:
- Silhouette, fit, proportions, garment length, sleeve/leg shape, and drape.
- Seams, topstitching, panel boundaries, ribbing, cuffs, waistband, hood, drawstrings, zippers, buttons, pockets, and hardware.
- Fabric grain, knit/weave, fleece pile, denim texture, satin sheen, leather grain, wash, fading, distressing, and natural folds.
- Original garment color and color relationships between panels or trims.
- Existing print/embroidery scale, location, texture, and edge quality.

## Allowed cleanup when requested

- Dust, sensor spots, stray threads, temporary background debris.
- Uneven background, minor lighting imbalance, distracting reflections.
- Small exposure/white-balance corrections that do not alter the garment's true color.
- Minor wrinkles only when they are clearly unwanted and the user authorizes smoothing. Preserve natural drape and construction lines.

## Workflow

1. Identify garment boundaries and background separately.
2. Duplicate source; use masks and adjustment layers.
3. Clean background independently from garment pixels.
4. Correct white balance/exposure using neutral reference areas when available. Do not assume a white background means the garment should be brightened.
5. Remove dust or stray objects with local retouching. Sample texture from a nearby equivalent area, not from a different fabric panel.
6. Keep edge transitions natural; avoid cutout halos and over-sharpened seams.
7. Compare garment color and texture against the original.
8. Export the requested format and retain transparency only when requested.

## Product image checks

- Background is clean without eating into garment edges.
- No missing drawstrings, seams, stitching, or hardware.
- No plastic-looking fabric or excessive smoothing.
- No unintended color shift or loss of wash/distressing.
- Garment proportions and folds remain credible.
